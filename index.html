I’m a first-year undergraduate studying Applied and Computational Mathematics at the University of Southern California. I’m interested in video reasoning, world models, and applied mathematics. I explore how models reason through visual representations, predict a changing world, and maintain a coherent understanding of objects and physical interactions.

I co-first authored Training Object Permanence in World Models (WROP), which studies object permanence and solidity in video generation through cognitively grounded tasks, scalable synthetic data, and evaluation.

Can a video model remember what it can no longer see? WROP studies whether models preserve object identity through occlusion and respect the solidity of physical objects.

Research overview

WROP brings together 150 cognitively grounded 3D tasks, a 1.5M-sample corpus, a fixed 300-question exam, a human evaluation framework, and PWM-WROP, a fine-tuned 16B continuation model. These are collaborative resources developed by the author team.
